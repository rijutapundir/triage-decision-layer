Learning, live

Each click draws one week of outcomes from a home decor store's simulated true response (fixed seed). Triage updates what it believes each action adds, starting from its built-in assumptions as a weak prior. Holdout checkouts teach the no-contact rate; explore, Triage and default checkouts teach each action's rate.

Learned estimates live only in this panel: the decision feed and its four note cases keep the built-in assumptions. Each week splits checkouts 10% holdout, 10% explore, 40% Triage, 40% default; the margin comparison uses realised outcomes only.

For evaluators: why one default isn't enough

Simulated across four merchant types. Merchants never see other merchants' data.

Triage's decisions are made with noisy lift estimates (early: high noise, 10% explore; mature: lower noise, 5% explore) and scored against each merchant's true response.

Simulated
Across 20 simulated worlds, the best single default's worst merchant averaged 83% of its best fixed rule (range 59–100%). Triage's worst averaged 90% in its first weeks (ahead of the default's worst in 12 of 20, so it starts on the default) and 96% once its estimates matured (ahead in 19 of 20).

Is the pilot big enough? Simulated

To detect a 4pp lift over a 14.5% baseline with 95% confidence and 80% power, the holdout needs about 1,230 cases. At ~400 failed checkouts a day and a 10% holdout, that takes about 31 days. Before that, Outcomes shows assumptions, not results.
